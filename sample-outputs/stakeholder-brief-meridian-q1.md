## The situation

Three of our six product teams (Workspace, Directory, Finance) build most new features from Meridian, our shared library of interface components and design standards. The other three are largely building their own: Mobile and Analytics use it for 20–30% of their components, and Integrations for under 5%. Those teams are rebuilding pieces that already exist, and similar features look and behave differently depending on which team built them.

Meridian is at the Managed stage: the components are well built and documented, but how teams contribute to it, retire old parts, and track who uses it all happens informally. That informality is what is stopping the teams that don't use it from relying on it.

## Why this matters

Every component a team rebuilds is engineering time not spent on the product, and the rebuilt versions don't get the fixes the shared ones do. When Workspace improves the shared Button for accessibility, Mobile's copy doesn't change. We can't yet say how much time this costs: [needs data: engineering time the three low-adoption teams spend building and maintaining duplicate components]. Nobody tracks adoption today, and that gap is part of what this brief asks to fix.

For customers, the inconsistency shows up as the same task looking different in the mobile and web apps, which reads as a lack of polish and prompts "is this a bug?" support questions.

## What we recommend

**Give each low-adoption team one engineering day per sprint for system integration over the next two quarters, starting with discovery.** The first sprint finds out why each team diverged, whether that's missing components, fit or preference, and then migrates the highest-risk components first (authentication, data entry).

The alternative, mandatory adoption, would be faster on paper but would push teams onto components that may not fit their needs, which is the likely reason they diverged. We'd rather fix the gaps and keep intentional local work documented.

## What we need

- **Decision:** treat Meridian as shared infrastructure that teams are expected to build on, not an optional toolkit.
- **Capacity:** one engineering day per sprint from Mobile, Analytics and Integrations for two quarters: 0.1 FTE per team with two-week sprints (1 day in 10), so 0.3 FTE in total, from existing headcount. [needs data: confirm sprint length; with one-week sprints this is 0.2 FTE per team, 0.6 in total]
- **Governance time:** about 12 hours of design systems team time to write down the contribution and deprecation processes (4 and 8 hours in the health assessment's action list), plus a monthly adoption dashboard (6 hours) so progress can be measured.

## Expected outcome

By the end of Q2, the three teams will be building new work with shared components wherever the system fits them, with any local exceptions written down as decisions. The adoption dashboard gives us a measured baseline and a way to report progress next quarter. This won't remove existing inconsistencies overnight; it stops new ones accumulating.
